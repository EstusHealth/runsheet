Boundaries — your takeaway sheet

From the live session with Liam & Nic · Estus Health

The one-liner: Boundaries are self-defined limits that protect your core needs and values across the domains of your life.

The big reframe

A boundary is about what you do, not about controlling anyone else. Think "If X, then I will Y."

Valuethe "why"

The enduring principle underneath (health, honesty, family). Boundaries protect values.

Rulecontrols others

"You may not do that." An attempt to control someone else. Not a boundary.

Preferencenegotiable

Low-stakes, tradeable. "I'd prefer Thai tonight." Nice to have, easy to flex.

Requestasks them

"Can you pick me up?" You're asking for a behavior — they can say no.

Boundaryself-defined + your action

"If you start yelling at me, I'll leave and go home." You define the limit and your own response to it.

The continuum

It's a spectrum, not a personality type. The goal is the middle — clear yet flexible.

Too open

Porous

Can't say "no", over-discloses, makes excuses for disrespect.

The target

Healthy

Clear, protects core values, stated assertively — and adjusts to context.

Too closed

Rigid

Keeps everyone at arm's length, refuses help, ghosts as a shield.

The 6 steps

1

Awareness

Find where you need one. Red flags: resentment, ambiguity, and the energy drain you keep repeating.

2

State it clearly

Assertive, not aggressive: "When you [do X], I feel [Y], and I need [Z]."

3

Consistency

Any boundary that's defined will be tested. Follow through — that's where trust is built.

4

Anticipate pushback

Guilt trips, picking fights, ghosting. Decide your response in advance.

5

Adapt

Revise openly, and define exceptions up front ("no calls after 10pm — unless it's an emergency").

6

Manage the guilt

Expect it. Guilt is not proof you did something wrong.

"Boundaries are the ultimate act of love — not just for your own health and safety, but for the other person's."

The 8 domains

Boundaries live everywhere. Most people are strong in some domains and nearly invisible in others — where's your weakest?

1 · Physical

Space & touch

Healthy: "Please don't make her hug anyone."

2 · Relational

People-pleasing

Healthy: notice the pause before you say yes.

3 · Emotional

Your feelings vs theirs

Healthy: "I care, but I'm not responsible for how you feel."

4 · Time

Where your hours go

Healthy: "No work email after I leave the office."

5 · Identity

Being your true self

Healthy: "That won't work for me anymore."

6 · Intellectual

Ideas & debate

Healthy: choose which debates are worth your energy.

7 · Digital

Devices & availability

Healthy: Do-Not-Disturb at 7pm; "I'm a slow texter."

8 · Financial

Money & obligations

Healthy: an explicit "who pays for what" conversation.

Scripts — saying no without guilt or drama

You don't need the perfect words — you need a few you can reuse. Tap Copy and keep them somewhere handy.

The core formula (NVC)
"When you [do X], I feel [Y]. What I need is [Z]."
The clean no
"Thanks for thinking of me — I'm not able to take this on."
No, without a reason
"That doesn't work for me."
Buy the pause
"Let me check and get back to you."
The if-then boundary
"If [behavior] keeps happening, I'll [your action]."
Family / holidays
"That won't work for me anymore — but here's what I can do…"
Work / time
"I don't check email after 6 — I'll reply first thing tomorrow."
Money
"I'm not able to lend money, but I'm happy to help you think it through."
Deflect the guilt-trip
"I understand you're disappointed — and my answer is still no."

Your one challenge

Pick ONE boundary. Write its if-then.

"If [behavior] keeps happening, I will [your action]."

Say it out loud once. Then say it to the person it's for. Any boundary that's defined will be tested — follow through, and expect the guilt without obeying it.
